# ARV Tech67 Protected Commercial Surface Boundary v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the protected commercial surface boundary for ARV and ProofOps.

It does not authorize publication, release, packaging, repository exposure, public API release or external distribution.

Its purpose is to define which ARV business, orchestration, certification, dispute, telemetry, managed-service and customer-specific surfaces must remain protected from any future open-source candidate.

The document preserves a deliberately minimized public candidate surface while protecting the commercial system that gives ARV operational, evidentiary and market value.

## 2. Governing Doctrine

ARV preserves the foundational distinction:

**Proof != Authority**

Cryptographic evidence may establish integrity, traceability and provenance within the scope of captured evidence. It does not automatically establish legal validity, judicial authority, commercial entitlement, certification authority or customer-facing service authority.

The canonical local kernel remains governed by the **LOCAL_L0** isolation doctrine.

No public candidate may transform a protected commercial system into public material by inertia, convenience, tooling default, repository structure, packaging pressure, deployment automation or documentation drift.

## 4. Boundary Definitions

### 4.1 Public Candidate Surface

The public candidate surface is the deliberately minimized portion of ARV that may be considered for future public release after separate review.

It may include neutral, reproducible, technically separable and independently auditable material.

A public candidate surface is not a product authority surface.

A public candidate surface is not a commercial service surface.

A public candidate surface is not a certification authority surface.

A public candidate surface is not a managed customer workflow surface.

### 4.2 Protected Commercial Surface

The Protected Commercial Surface is the business, product, service, orchestration, certification, dispute, telemetry, managed infrastructure, customer workflow and authority layer that must remain private, managed or separately governed.

Protected Commercial Surface material includes any component that creates, prices, routes, scores, issues, validates, sells, manages, disputes, measures or operationalizes ARV commercial value.

### 4.3 Customer-Specific Operational Surface

Customer-specific operational surface means any customer, enterprise, agency, freelancer, dispute, delivery, evidence, proof-chain or workflow material that is tied to a real user, real transaction, real matter, real operational record or real commercial engagement.

Customer-specific operational surface is protected by default.

## 6. Public Candidate Surface Rule

A future public candidate must be deliberately minimized.

The public surface may be prepared only around:

- neutral technical specifications;
- public data schemas;
- deterministic verification examples;
- reproducible test vectors;
- portable local verification interfaces;
- narrowly scoped LOCAL_L0 verification logic;
- public-facing documentation expressly approved for release;
- license notices;
- attribution notices;
- reproducible local smoke tests;
- neutral sample artifacts that do not expose protected implementation details.

Public material must not expose production orchestration, commercial decision logic, privileged execution paths, customer-specific operational systems, private dispute strategy, private managed-service logic, certification authority mechanisms, signing infrastructure or credentialed connectors.
