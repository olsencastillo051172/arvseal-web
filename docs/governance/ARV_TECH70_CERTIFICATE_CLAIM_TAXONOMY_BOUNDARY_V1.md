# ARV Tech70 Certificate Claim Taxonomy Boundary v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the certificate claim taxonomy boundary for ARV and ProofOps.

It defines which claims may be expressed by ARV certificate-adjacent material, public validation surfaces, machine-readable outputs, documentation, demos or future controlled exports.

This document does not authorize publication, release, deployment, packaging, repository exposure, public API exposure, official Evidence Certificate issuance, Evidence Certificate sale, Bundle Evidence Package sale, Dispute Pack sale or external distribution.

Its purpose is to prevent accidental overclaiming.

The governing principle remains:

**Proof != Authority**

## 2. Governing Doctrine

Cryptographic evidence may establish integrity, traceability, provenance and internal consistency within the scope of captured evidence.

Public validation may confirm that a bounded artifact is technically verifiable.

Official ARV certificate issuance is different.

No public verifier, open-source candidate, local tool, demo, public API, public repository, public document, automated output or deployment surface may represent itself as legal authority, judicial authority, certification authority, commercial entitlement authority, customer-facing service authority or official ARV issuance authority.

## 3. Current Controlled-Export Status

Current controlled-export status:

**NO-GO**

The project may continue documentation, private validation, controlled inventory preparation, controlled extraction planning and protection-track execution.

No OSS publication, package release, public artifact, public API release, commercial certificate issuance or external distribution is authorized by this document.

## 4. Claim Taxonomy Boundary

ARV claim language is divided into controlled classes.

A public or open-source candidate may only use claim language that remains inside the allowed technical-verification boundary.

A protected or official ARV commercial surface may use higher-authority claim language only after separate governance approval.

## 5. Allowed Technical Claims

A public candidate surface may state that an artifact is:

- hashed;
- timestamped;
- locally recorded;
- internally consistent;
- cryptographically verifiable;
- reproducible from the supplied artifact;
- traceable within the supplied record;
- bound to a disclosed digest;
- associated with a disclosed metadata record;
- verifiable against a supplied manifest;
- verifiable against a supplied proof package;
- generated by a disclosed local process;
- checked against a declared validation rule.

These claims are technical claims only.

They do not create legal, commercial, judicial, certification or institutional authority.

## 6. Restricted Authority Claims

A public candidate surface must not claim that an artifact is:

- legally valid;
- court-admissible;
- judicially certified;
- officially certified by ARV;
- commercially certified by ARV;
- dispute-ready by default;
- accepted by any institution;
- accepted by any platform;
- accepted by any court;
- accepted by any regulator;
- accepted by any customer;
- endorsed by ARV as a paid commercial certificate;
- covered by an ARV Evidence Certificate;
- covered by a Bundle Evidence Package;
- covered by a Dispute Pack;
- protected by official ARV issuance authority;
- guaranteed as true;
- guaranteed as authentic in the material-world sense;
- guaranteed as fraud-free;
- guaranteed as complete;
- guaranteed as legally enforceable.

These claims remain protected.

## 7. Evidence Certificate Claim Boundary

An official ARV Evidence Certificate may eventually include controlled commercial claims only after separate approval.

Until such approval, no public candidate, OSS component, local demo, public verifier, static site, documentation page, machine-readable output or automation may represent itself as issuing an Evidence Certificate.

The phrase **Evidence Certificate** remains protected commercial terminology.

## 8. Bundle Evidence Package Claim Boundary

The phrase **Bundle Evidence Package** remains protected commercial terminology.

No public candidate may claim that it creates, sells, issues, packages, certifies, guarantees, adjudicates or validates a Bundle Evidence Package.

A public candidate may only describe technical packaging or local verification when expressly bounded as non-official and non-commercial.

## 9. Dispute Pack Claim Boundary

The phrase **Dispute Pack** remains protected commercial terminology.

No public candidate may claim that it creates, sells, issues, certifies, guarantees, adjudicates, validates or prepares an official Dispute Pack.

A public candidate may not imply dispute success, platform acceptance, court acceptance, legal sufficiency or procedural completeness.

## 10. Truth Boundary

ARV technical verification does not establish truth by itself.

A hash may prove that a file matches a digest.

A timestamp may prove that a record existed at a captured time.

A chain record may prove that a sequence was recorded.

These proofs do not automatically establish legal truth, factual truth, testimonial truth, authorship truth, customer acceptance, platform acceptance or judicial truth.

## 11. Authorship Boundary

A public candidate may not claim that a person authored, approved, signed, accepted or endorsed an artifact unless that fact is independently captured and bounded inside the supplied evidence record.

Even then, the claim must remain technical and evidentiary.

It must not become legal, institutional or commercial authority by implication.

## 12. Identity Boundary

A public candidate may not claim legal identity verification, official identity assurance, customer KYC completion, regulatory KYC completion or institutional identity acceptance.

A public candidate may only record bounded identifiers that were supplied to the process.

## 13. Time Boundary

A public candidate may claim that a local or disclosed timestamp exists in a record.

It may not claim official time authority, legal time authority, notarial timestamp authority or universal timestamp finality unless a separate approved timestamp authority is integrated and disclosed.

## 14. Machine-Readable Output Boundary

Machine-readable outputs must include bounded claim language.

They must not use fields that imply official ARV issuance, certification, adjudication, acceptance or legal authority.

Allowed fields may include:

- artifact_hash;
- hash_algorithm;
- generated_at;
- local_timestamp;
- manifest_digest;
- proof_record;
- verification_status;
- technical_scope;
- authority_boundary;
- non_authority_notice.

Protected fields include:

- official_certificate_id;
- evidence_certificate_id;
- certified_by_arv;
- legal_validity;
- court_admissibility;
- dispute_pack_status;
- bundle_evidence_package_status;
- commercial_entitlement;
- customer_acceptance;
- institutional_acceptance;
- official_issuance_status.

## 15. Public Wording Rule

Public wording must remain narrow.

Allowed pattern:

"This artifact is technically verifiable against the supplied hash, metadata and proof record."

Blocked pattern:

"This artifact is officially certified, legally valid, court-ready, dispute-ready or accepted."

## 16. Publication Gate

A future public candidate involving certificate-claim language remains blocked until the project has completed:

- explicit certificate claim taxonomy approval;
- public verifier wording review;
- Evidence Certificate terminology review;
- Bundle Evidence Package terminology review;
- Dispute Pack terminology review;
- authority wording review;
- machine-readable output boundary review;
- protected commercial terminology exclusion review;
- customer evidence exclusion approval;
- independent governance review;
- signed GO / NO-GO decision.

No technical artifact may bypass the claim taxonomy publication gate.

## 17. Audit Principle

ARV will not overclaim by accident.

Any future public validation or certificate-adjacent surface must be deliberately allowed, deliberately extracted, minimized, separately reviewed and independently auditable.

The protected certificate, bundle and dispute commercial system remains isolated behind the governance boundary.
