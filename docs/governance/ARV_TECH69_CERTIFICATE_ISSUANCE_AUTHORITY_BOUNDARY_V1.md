# ARV Tech69 Certificate Issuance Authority Boundary v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the certificate issuance authority boundary for ARV and ProofOps.

It defines what an official ARV Evidence Certificate may represent, who may issue it, and what public, open-source or externally visible verification surfaces must never imply, issue, certify, adjudicate or authorize.

This document does not authorize publication, release, packaging, repository exposure, public API release or external distribution of any protected ARV commercial system.

Its purpose is to preserve the distinction between cryptographic verification, public validation and official ARV certificate issuance.

The governing principle remains:

**Proof != Authority**

## 2. Governing Doctrine

Cryptographic evidence may establish integrity, traceability, provenance and internal consistency within the scope of captured evidence.

Public validation may confirm that a bounded artifact is technically verifiable.

Official ARV certificate issuance is different.

An official ARV Evidence Certificate is a controlled commercial artifact issued only through an authorized ARV certificate issuance path.

No public verifier, open-source candidate, local tool, demo, public API, public repository, public document, automated workflow or deployment surface may represent itself as official ARV certificate authority.

## 3. Current Controlled-Export Status

Current controlled-export status:

The project may continue documentation, private validation, controlled inventory preparation, controlled extraction planning and protection-track execution.

No OSS repository, public package, public artifact, public API release, official certificate issuance, commercial certificate service or external distribution is authorized by this document.

## 4. Certificate Issuance Boundary

An official ARV Evidence Certificate may only be issued by an authorized ARV-controlled issuance process.

The certificate issuance process must remain separate from:

- public validation;
- open-source verification;
- local proof generation;
- demo workflows;
- repository automation;
- deployment previews;
- public API responses;
- unaudited agents;
- external partner systems;
- customer-side self-attestation;
- third-party verification tools.

A successful technical proof does not automatically create an official ARV Evidence Certificate.

A valid hash does not automatically create certification authority.

A verified artifact does not automatically create legal authority, judicial authority, commercial entitlement or customer-facing ARV issuance authority.

## 5. Official Evidence Certificate

An official ARV Evidence Certificate is a controlled commercial output.

It may include bounded claims such as:

- artifact identifier;
- cryptographic hash;
- timestamp;
- verification method;
- evidence package reference;
- issuer boundary;
- issuance status;
- certificate serial;
- revocation status;
- verification URL;
- scope limitation;
- authority limitation;
- audit reference.

It must not imply authority beyond the explicitly bounded certificate scope.

## 6. Non-Authority Rule

No certificate may imply that ARV has adjudicated the underlying real-world truth of an event unless a separate authorized adjudication process exists and is explicitly invoked.

No certificate may imply that ARV is a court, regulator, notary, legal authority, judicial authority, tax authority, law-enforcement authority, licensing authority or certification authority unless that authority has been separately obtained and formally authorized.

ARV may certify its own controlled verification process.

ARV must not accidentally certify external truth, legal validity, ownership, authorship, identity, compliance, entitlement or liability outside the authorized certificate scope.

## 7. Public Verifier Limitation

A public verifier may validate bounded technical properties.

A public verifier must not issue:

- official ARV Evidence Certificates;
- official ARV Dispute Packs;
- official ARV Bundle Evidence Packages;
- official ARV Snap attestations;
- customer-facing certification decisions;
- legal conclusions;
- adjudication outcomes;
- commercial entitlement decisions;
- authority-bearing seals;
- privileged certificate metadata;
- private issuance records.

A public verifier may say that an artifact is technically consistent.

A public verifier must not say that ARV has officially certified the artifact unless the artifact was issued through the authorized certificate issuance process.

## 9. Commercial Boundary

Evidence Certificate is a commercial ARV product surface.

Bundle Evidence Package is a commercial ARV product surface.

Dispute Pack is a commercial ARV product surface.

Public validation tools may support trust in ARV proofs.

They must not expose, clone, replace or commoditize the protected certificate issuance system.

## 10. Certificate Claim Classes

ARV certificate claims must be bounded.

Permitted bounded claim classes may include:

- this artifact hash equals the recorded hash;
- this evidence package was produced under a recorded process;
- this certificate was issued by an ARV-controlled issuance path;
- this certificate serial exists;
- this certificate has not been marked revoked at the time of verification;
- this certificate references a defined evidence package;
- this certificate scope is limited to the recorded artifact and metadata.

Prohibited implied claim classes include:

- ARV has adjudicated real-world truth;
- ARV has determined legal liability;
- ARV has certified ownership;
- ARV has certified authorship;
- ARV has certified regulatory compliance;
- ARV has certified judicial admissibility;
- ARV has certified tax validity;
- ARV has certified identity beyond the verified credential scope;
- ARV has certified commercial entitlement beyond the certificate terms;
- ARV has become a public authority.

## 11. Machine-Readable Outputs

Machine-readable outputs must preserve the authority boundary.

Any future certificate JSON, API response, QR payload, manifest, verification receipt or proof response must include bounded fields that distinguish:

- proof status;
- validation status;
- certificate status;
- issuer status;
- authority status;
- scope limitation;
- revocation status;
- public/private surface classification.

No machine-readable output may allow downstream systems to infer official ARV certification from mere technical verification.

## 12. Signing Keys and Secrets

Certificate signing keys are protected commercial and security material.

No public candidate may include:

- signing keys;
- key-generation secrets;
- production credentials;
- certificate seal secrets;
- issuer private keys;
- token material;
- privileged connectors;
- protected environment variables;
- private certificate templates;
- production certificate configuration;
- private certificate validation endpoints.

## 13. Evidence Certificate vs Proof Receipt

A proof receipt may show that a file, event or evidence package is technically recorded.

An Evidence Certificate is an official ARV-issued commercial certificate.

The two surfaces must remain separate.

A proof receipt may support later certificate issuance.

A proof receipt must not become an official Evidence Certificate by naming, styling, metadata drift, UI copy, QR payload, API wording or deployment automation.

## 14. Dispute Pack Boundary

A Dispute Pack may reference Evidence Certificates and Bundle Evidence Packages.

A Dispute Pack remains a protected commercial product surface.

No public candidate may expose dispute automation logic, private argument templates, customer-specific evidence strategies, private legal routing logic, privileged dispute metadata or protected commercial workflow logic.

## 15. Publication Gate

A future public candidate involving certificate-adjacent material remains blocked until the project has completed:

- explicit certificate issuance boundary approval;
- public verifier wording review;
- certificate claim taxonomy review;
- signing-key exclusion review;
- secret and credential scanning;
- commercial surface exclusion approval;
- customer evidence exclusion approval;
- machine-readable output boundary review;
- independent governance review;
- signed GO / NO-GO decision.

No technical artifact may bypass the certificate issuance publication gate.

## 16. Audit Principle

ARV will not create official certificate authority by accident.

Any future public validation surface must be deliberately allowed, deliberately extracted, minimized, separately reviewed and independently auditable.

The protected certificate issuance system remains isolated behind the governance boundary.
