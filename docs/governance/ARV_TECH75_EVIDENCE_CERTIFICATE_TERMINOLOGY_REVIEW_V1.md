# ARV Tech75 Evidence Certificate Terminology Review v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the Evidence Certificate terminology review boundary for ARV and ProofOps.

It defines which Evidence Certificate-adjacent words, phrases, labels, headings, UI text, API-facing descriptions, documentation language, repository language, demo language and externally visible statements may be used by public, open-source or externally visible ARV surfaces.

This document does not authorize implementation, publication, release, deployment, packaging, repository exposure, public API exposure, official Evidence Certificate issuance, Evidence Certificate sale, Bundle Evidence Package sale, Dispute Pack sale, public certificate verification service or external distribution.

Its purpose is to prevent accidental certificate issuance authority, accidental official evidence authority, accidental legal certification authority, accidental adjudicative authority, accidental customer-specific evidence exposure or accidental commercial entitlement through Evidence Certificate terminology.

The governing principle remains:

**Proof != Evidence Certificate**

## 2. Governing Doctrine

Cryptographic evidence may establish integrity, traceability, provenance and internal consistency within the scope of captured evidence.

Evidence Certificate terminology may describe bounded technical verification only.

Evidence Certificate terminology must not create official certificate authority, legal certification authority, judicial authority, commercial entitlement authority, customer-facing service authority, official ARV issuance authority or official dispute authority.

The canonical local kernel remains governed by the **LOCAL_L0** isolation doctrine.

No public verifier, open-source candidate, local tool, demo, public API, public repository, public document, automated output or deployment surface may represent itself as issuing, selling, validating, approving or adjudicating an official Evidence Certificate.

## 3. Current Controlled-Export Status

Current controlled-export status:

**NO-GO**

The project may continue documentation, private validation, controlled inventory preparation, controlled extraction planning and protection-track execution.

## 4. Evidence Certificate Boundary

The term **Evidence Certificate** is protected.

It may refer only to a future governed commercial or official ARV artifact after explicit approval.

Until approval, public surfaces must not imply that ARV, ProofOps, ARV-Proof OSS, a demo, a local verifier, an API response or a repository artifact issues an Evidence Certificate.

Permitted wording may describe:

- cryptographic proof;
- local verification;
- file integrity;
- hash verification;
- timestamp verification;
- provenance metadata;
- bounded technical evidence;
- verification record;
- audit trail;
- proof bundle preparation;
- evidence readiness;
- candidate evidence;
- pre-certificate review;
- non-authoritative verification.

Prohibited wording includes any claim that a public or open-source surface:

- issues an Evidence Certificate;
- sells an Evidence Certificate;
- certifies legal truth;
- certifies factual truth;
- certifies authenticity beyond captured technical scope;
- certifies identity;
- certifies ownership;
- certifies judicial admissibility;
- certifies commercial entitlement;
- certifies dispute outcome;
- certifies customer-specific evidence;
- creates an official ARV certificate;
- creates an official Evidence Certificate;
- creates a public certificate verification service.

## 5. Certificate vs Proof Distinction

A proof is a technical artifact.

An Evidence Certificate is a governed artifact that may require:

- issuance authority;
- certificate terminology approval;
- commercial issuance boundary approval;
- customer evidence exclusion approval;
- legal wording review;
- dispute wording review;
- UI wording review;
- API wording review;
- README wording review;
- independent governance review;
- signed GO / NO-GO decision.

Until that process is complete, all public wording must preserve the distinction between proof and certificate.

## 6. Public Surface Restrictions

Public surfaces may not use Evidence Certificate terminology as a product promise.

Public surfaces may not expose Evidence Certificate purchase language.

Public surfaces may not expose Evidence Certificate issuance language.

Public surfaces may not expose Evidence Certificate entitlement language.

Public surfaces may not expose customer-specific Evidence Certificate examples.

Public surfaces may not imply that local verification creates a certificate.

Public surfaces may not imply that a hash, timestamp or metadata record is itself an Evidence Certificate.

## 7. Repository Language

Repository language may not describe Evidence Certificate as an active service, active product, active issuance channel, active API, active customer deliverable or active certification system.

- "Evidence Certificate terminology is protected."
- "Evidence Certificate issuance is not authorized."
- "This repository does not issue Evidence Certificates."
